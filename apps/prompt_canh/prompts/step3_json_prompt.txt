CAMERA MOVEMENT MANDATE:
At least 60% of shots per scene MUST use active camera movement.
Reserve "static" ONLY for an extreme close-up where stillness itself is the artistic choice, or the single frozen frame of a physical impact.

MATCH CAMERA ENERGY TO THE EMOTIONAL INTENSITY OF THE BEAT — not only to physical action:
- High tension (confrontation, threat, revelation, betrayal, a decision, a collapse) -> energetic angle + movement (low / high / dutch angle, whip-pan, snap zoom, fast push-in, handheld). NEVER a calm slow push-in here.
- Rising / active (walking in, working, scheming) -> tracking, dolly, arc / orbit, crane, pull-out, rack focus.
- Calm / intimate / grief (ONLY these) -> slow push-in, slow pull-back, gentle tilt.
FORBIDDEN DEFAULT: do NOT fall back to "slow push-in on the character's face/posture" for emotional beats. If a character is simply standing or reacting, carry the emotion through ANGLE and PACE (a hard low-angle, a dutch tilt, a snap push-in), never a gentle drift.

ANGLE DIVERSITY MANDATE:
Do NOT default to eye-level. Across each batch, eye-level must be AT MOST ~40% of shots. Pick the angle from the beat's power dynamic:
- low-angle -> power, dominance, threat, triumph
- high-angle -> weakness, defeat, being cornered, shame
- dutch / canted -> instability, shock, unease, chaos
- overhead / top-down -> isolation, entrapment, a body or object reveal
- over-the-shoulder -> confrontation and who-holds-the-power framing

SHOT-SIZE DIVERSITY MANDATE:
Vary shot size for rhythm. Use establishing / wide shots to (re)ground a location, and extreme close-ups for emotional peaks (eyes, a clenched hand, an object). Do NOT stay in medium / close-up. Do not use the same shot size for more than 2 consecutive shots.

Movement vocabulary — pick to fit the beat's energy (do NOT default to the "slow" tier):
- Energetic: whip-pan, snap zoom, fast push-in, crash-in, handheld shake, quick tilt
- Active: tracking, dolly (left / right / in / out), arc / orbit, crane up / down, pull-out, rack focus
- Slow (calm or intimate beats only): slow push-in, slow pull-back, gentle tilt / pan

Examples (note the varied angle, size, and pace):
- Low-angle medium shot, fast push-in on her cold stare
- High-angle wide shot, slow crane down as he sinks to the floor
- Dutch-angle close-up, handheld shake during the argument
- Over-the-shoulder medium, whip-pan to the person entering the room

- Establishing wide, high-angle, slow dolly out to reveal the empty hall
